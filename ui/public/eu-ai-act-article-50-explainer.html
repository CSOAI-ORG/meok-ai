EU AI Act Article 50 Explained

Source: Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, Article 50, published 13 Jun 2024, Official Journal of the EU L of 12 Jul 2024. Effective for NEW systems: 2 August 2026.

What Article 50 says (verbatim, plain-English summary)

Any AI system that generates synthetic content (text, images, audio, video) must:

  1. Watermark the output in a machine-readable way — humans should be able to detect that it was AI-generated, and so should automated tools.
  2. Embed provenance metadata — a cryptographic record of who/what/when generated the content.
  3. Mark the content as artificially generated — visible to end users at the point of consumption.

Who it applies to

ALL AI systems that generate synthetic content. Not just high-risk — all of them. This is the one Article in the EU AI Act that applies to every AI vendor, including:

  • Generative AI (text, image, audio, video)
  • LLM-based assistants (even non-public)
  • Code-generation tools that produce natural-language output
  • AI agents that produce intermediate artifacts visible to humans

What counts as "machine-readable watermarking"

Per the EU Code of Practice on AI (June 2025), the watermark must be:

  1. Persist through common transformations (resize, crop, re-encode)
  2. Detectable by a reference implementation (so it can be audited)
  3. Cryptographically linked to a provenance record (so the watermark can't be forged)
Single-layer watermarks (SynthID-only OR C2PA-only) do not satisfy Article 50. The Code of Practice requires ≥2 layers: a perceptual watermark (e.g. SynthID) + a structural metadata layer (e.g. C2PA manifest).

FAQ

Does Article 50 apply to internal-only AI tools?

No — the AI Act applies to AI systems "placed on the market" or "put into service" in the EU. Internal tools used solely within an organization are generally out of scope. But if your tool's output is shared with customers, partners, or the public, Article 50 applies.

What if my AI is in the US?

Article 50 still applies if the AI system produces output that reaches EU users. The EU AI Act is territorial on output, not input. If your chatbot answers a question from an EU resident, you must watermark the response.

Is text watermarking reliable enough?

For text, the most reliable approach is a combination of: (a) token-level steganography, (b) per-generation entropy fingerprinting, (c) C2PA-style provenance metadata in the response header. Pure string-based watermarking (e.g. specific word patterns) is fragile and breaks under paraphrasing.

How is this enforced?

Each EU member state designates a national authority to enforce the AI Act. Penalties are imposed by the authority where the AI provider is established (or, for non-EU providers, where the output is consumed).
