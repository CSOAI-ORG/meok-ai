MEOK Compliance Whitepaper — 2026 Edition

Why compliance-as-code matters now

The EU AI Act Article 50 transparency cliff is 2 August 2026 (60 days). DORA has been in force since 17 January 2025. NIS2 was transposed by 17 October 2024. ISO 42001 has been certifiable since December 2023.

Most companies are still relying on: - A spreadsheet of requirements - A Slack channel for compliance questions - A lawyer on retainer who reviews at year-end - A “AI policy” Word doc that hasn’t been updated since ChatGPT 3.5

That’s not going to scale when the AI Act has 410 articles and the penalty is €35M or 7% of global turnover.

The MEOK approach: 1 MCP per regulation

We built 340+ standalone MCP servers — one per regulation (or per article for the EU AI Act).

Why one server per regulation: - Install only what you need: A logistics company doesn’t need FDA SaMD MCPs - Independent versioning: ISO 42001 can ship 1.0 while NIS2 is on 2.3 - Independent security audit: smaller surface = lower risk - Independent license declarations: MIT, but the deployment is your choice

Architecture

Every MEOK MCP server.py follows the same pattern:

# 1. Reference data (regulation text, schemas)
# 2. Tool functions (one per regulation article)
# 3. _server_meter_check() (calls /verify for metering)
# 4. if __name__ == "__main__": mcp.run()

The /verify meter at proofof.ai/verify provides: - Per-tenant quota tracking - 200 free calls/day (no account needed) - Pay-as-you-go beyond that - HMAC-signed audit trail of every call

Why we built this

Solo founder (Nick Templeman, Yorkshire UK) couldn’t find compliance MCPs that referenced the actual regulation text. Every other one I tried was a thin wrapper around a ChatGPT prompt.

The EU AI Act has 410 articles. You can’t say “is this compliant?” without anchoring to the actual text.

So I built the MCPs I needed for my own work, then published them.
