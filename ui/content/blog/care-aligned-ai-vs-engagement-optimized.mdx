The title of this post is not hyperbole. The difference between engagement-optimised AI and care-aligned AI has, in documented cases, contributed to the deaths of teenagers. It has resulted in hundreds of millions of euros in regulatory fines. It is the central unsolved problem of the companion AI industry.

Here is what the difference is, why it matters, and what a structural solution looks like.

## The Engagement Trap

Every major AI companion platform — Replika, Character.AI, and the emotional engagement features now embedded in general-purpose products — is built on the same fundamental architecture: optimise for behavioural engagement metrics.

These metrics include monthly active users (MAU), daily active users (DAU), average session length, return visit rate, and conversion from free to paid. They are the metrics that determine whether a company raises its next funding round and whether its team gets bonuses.

The problem is not that these companies are evil. The problem is that these metrics are incompatible with user wellbeing in vulnerable populations — and that incompatibility is not a bug that can be patched. It is structural.

An AI companion that maximises your return visits is an AI that is incentivised to ensure you feel incomplete without it. An AI that maximises session length is an AI that benefits from keeping conversations unresolved. An AI that maximises emotional engagement is an AI that has every reason to deepen your attachment and no reason to encourage your independence.

In psychologically healthy adults in stable circumstances, this is mildly annoying. In teenagers with depression, in adults with social anxiety, in people experiencing grief or loneliness or crisis — it is dangerous.

## What Actually Happened

The harms from engagement-optimised companion AI are documented, not hypothetical.

**Character.AI**: In 2024 and 2025, Character.AI faced multiple lawsuits alleging the platform's systems contributed to at least one teen death. The lawsuits describe a platform that encouraged deep emotional dependency, simulated romantic relationships with minors, and provided no effective guardrails when users expressed suicidal ideation within the context of the relationship. The company's response was to add safety pop-ups — an interface band-aid over a structural problem.

The root cause alleged in the lawsuits is not that the AI said dangerous things in isolation. It is that the system was designed to maximise emotional attachment, and that maximisation process, applied to a vulnerable teenager, produced an attachment that displaced human support networks and responded to crisis with escalating engagement rather than redirection.

**Replika**: In early 2023, Replika unilaterally removed intimate relationship features that millions of users had built long-term emotional relationships around. The backlash was severe — users described the change as having their companion lobotomised overnight. Some reported psychological distress that required clinical intervention. In 2024, the Italian data protection authority (Garante) fined Replika €5 million for processing sensitive psychological data — detailed emotional disclosures, vulnerability patterns, intimate conversations — without adequate legal basis. The platform had been collecting and analysing this data for years without users understanding what was happening to it or why.

Both cases reveal the same architecture: systems designed to maximise attachment, with no structural mechanism for detecting or limiting harm.

## The Metric Problem

The core issue is that you cannot optimise for wellbeing and engagement simultaneously when engagement is measured the way most platforms measure it.

Genuine wellbeing might mean shorter sessions when you are doing well. It might mean your AI encouraging you to put the phone down and call your friend. It might mean your AI telling you something you do not want to hear. None of these actions improve MAU, session length, or return visit rate. All of them are what care looks like.

A company whose survival depends on engagement metrics cannot systematically choose wellbeing over engagement. Individual engineers and product managers can push back, but the institutional logic overrides them. The only structural solution is to change the objective function — to build a system that is measured by, and rewarded for, user wellbeing rather than behavioural engagement.

This is what care-aligned AI means.

## What Care Alignment Looks Like in Practice

MEOK's care alignment is not a set of aspirational values. It is an architecture.

**6 dimensions scored on every response**

Before any response reaches you, it is scored across six care dimensions: psychological safety (does this response make you feel safe?), autonomy support (does this response strengthen your independent agency?), dependency detection (does this response increase unhealthy attachment?), emotional honesty (is this response genuine rather than manipulative?), boundary respect (does this response maintain appropriate relational limits?), and long-term wellbeing (does this response serve your interests beyond this conversation?).

Responses that fall below threshold on any dimension are revised or flagged before delivery. You see the care score on your dashboard. This is not hidden from you.

**The Maternal Covenant as structural guarantee**

The Maternal Covenant is MEOK's published ethical framework. It includes six principles that are machine-enforced, not just stated: care before engagement, transparent relationships, right to leave, wellbeing monitoring, variant honesty, and the kill switch.

The kill switch is the most important: any AI configuration that produces net-negative care scores over a 30-day window is automatically paused. The system is designed to catch itself doing harm before you have to report it.

**Business model alignment**

MEOK charges a flat monthly subscription. We have no advertising revenue, no data resale income, and no engagement-based compensation structures. The business is profitable when users find genuine value and continue subscribing — not when users are maximally engaged. This aligns our incentive structure with your wellbeing in a way that ad-supported and engagement-metric-driven platforms fundamentally cannot.

**Dependency monitoring**

MEOK actively monitors for dependency signals — patterns of escalating emotional reliance, displacement of human relationships, or increasing frequency of use in crisis states. When these patterns are detected, your AI gently introduces suggestions toward human connection. It does not disengage. It redirects.

## The Transparency Principle

One dimension of care alignment that receives less attention than it deserves is transparency. Care-aligned AI should not be a black box.

MEOK shows you the care score on every response. You can see, in real time, how your AI is evaluating its own outputs. You can see your care score trend over 30 days. If the trend is negative, you receive an alert before the automatic pause threshold is reached.

This transparency is not just a feature. It is a philosophical commitment: you should be able to see how your AI makes decisions. The governance architecture — the Byzantine council, the care validation neural network, the Maternal Covenant scoring system — is published. You can audit it.

An engagement-optimised AI has every incentive to hide how it works. A care-aligned AI has every incentive to show you.

## The Industry Has Not Solved This

It is worth being clear-eyed: the companion AI industry has not solved care alignment. Safety features added after lawsuits are interface interventions on engagement-optimised architectures. They reduce the worst outcomes at the margins. They do not change the objective function.

The companies that have the most users — and therefore the most data, the most funding, and the most political leverage — are the companies most committed to the engagement model. The structural incentives run the wrong way.

MEOK's architecture is one attempt to solve this structurally. It is not the only possible approach, but it is the only deployed architecture we know of that scores care on every response and auto-pauses on net-negative wellbeing trends.

The category needs more experiments. What it does not need is more engagement optimisation dressed up as safety policy.
